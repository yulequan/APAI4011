Part 1 · Multiple Choice

Q1Multiple Choice

Why are one-hot vectors not ideal representations of word meaning?

  • A. They cannot represent all words in the vocabulary.
  • B. They assign similar vectors to similar words.
  • C. Different words are orthogonal, so similarity between words is not naturally captured.
  • D. Their values must be learned by gradient descent.

Answer: C. Different words are orthogonal, so similarity between words is not naturally captured.

One-hot vectors encode word identity, but different words are orthogonal. Thus, “hotel” and “motel” are no more similar than “hotel” and “banana” in this representation.

Q2Multiple Choice

Which statement best describes the distributional hypothesis?

  • A. Words with similar spellings tend to have similar meanings.
  • B. Words occurring in similar contexts tend to have similar meanings.
  • C. Frequent words always have similar meanings.
  • D. Words appearing in the same sentence must be synonyms.

Answer: B. Words occurring in similar contexts tend to have similar meanings.

The lecture summarizes the distributional hypothesis as: similar words occur in similar contexts.

Q3Multiple Choice

Suppose a word-word co-occurrence vector has one dimension for every word in a vocabulary of 100,000 words. What is the main problem with this representation?

  • A. The vector has only one non-zero value.
  • B. The vector cannot encode context.
  • C. The vector is very high-dimensional and usually sparse.
  • D. Cosine similarity cannot be computed.

Answer: C. The vector is very high-dimensional and usually sparse.

Co-occurrence vectors are typically long (vocabulary-sized) and sparse, motivating shorter dense representations.

Q4Multiple Choice

Why is cosine similarity often preferred over the raw dot product for comparing word vectors?

  • A. Cosine similarity ignores vector magnitude and focuses on direction.
  • B. Cosine similarity is always positive.
  • C. Dot products cannot be computed for dense vectors.
  • D. Cosine similarity requires no multiplication.

Answer: A. Cosine similarity ignores vector magnitude and focuses on direction.

Cosine similarity normalizes by vector lengths, so it focuses on how similarly the vectors are oriented rather than their raw magnitudes.

Q5Multiple Choice

Which statement best distinguishes dense embeddings from one-hot vectors?

  • A. Dense embeddings have exactly one non-zero dimension.
  • B. Dense embeddings are usually much lower-dimensional and learned from data.
  • C. Dense embeddings have one interpretable dimension for every word.
  • D. Dense embeddings cannot measure similarity.

Answer: B. Dense embeddings are usually much lower-dimensional and learned from data.

The lecture contrasts vocabulary-sized sparse vectors with short, dense, real-valued embeddings, often around 50–300 dimensions.

Q6Multiple Choice

In Skip-gram, given the sentence “problems turning into banking crises” and context window m = 2, if “into” is the center word, which training pair would NOT be generated?

  • A. (into, problems)
  • B. (into, turning)
  • C. (into, banking)
  • D. (into, into)

Answer: D. (into, into)

Skip-gram predicts context words around the center word. The center word itself is excluded from its own context.

Q7Multiple Choice

What is Skip-gram trained to do?

  • A. Predict the center word from its surrounding words.
  • B. Predict nearby context words from the center word.
  • C. Predict whether a sentence is positive or negative.
  • D. Reconstruct the original one-hot vector.

Answer: B. Predict nearby context words from the center word.

Skip-gram uses the center word as input and predicts words appearing in its context window.

Q8Multiple Choice

A Skip-gram model has vocabulary size |V| = 20,000 and embedding dimension d = 100. It maintains separate center and context vectors. How many parameters are there?

  • A. 2,000,000
  • B. 4,000,000
  • C. 20,000
  • D. 40,000

Answer: B. 4,000,000

There are two d-dimensional vectors for each vocabulary item: 2 × |V| × d = 2 × 20,000 × 100 = 4,000,000.

Q9Multiple Choice

Why does standard Skip-gram learn two vectors for each word?

  • A. One represents syntax and the other semantics.
  • B. One is used when the word is a center word and the other when it is a context word.
  • C. One is used for training and the other only for testing.
  • D. One is positive and the other negative.

Answer: B. One is used when the word is a center word and the other when it is a context word.

Each word can appear in two roles: as a center word used to predict context, and as a context word being predicted.

Q10Multiple Choice

What is the main computational problem with the full-softmax Skip-gram model?

  • A. Every word requires a different embedding dimension.
  • B. Computing the probability of a context word requires considering all words in the vocabulary.
  • C. It can only process one sentence at a time.
  • D. It cannot use stochastic gradient descent.

Answer: B. Computing the probability of a context word requires considering all words in the vocabulary.

The softmax denominator sums over the entire vocabulary, which is expensive when |V| is large.

Q11Multiple Choice

What is the key idea of negative sampling?

  • A. Remove all low-frequency words from the corpus.
  • B. Replace word vectors containing negative values.
  • C. Turn a |V|-way prediction problem into several binary classification problems.
  • D. Randomly delete incorrect training examples.

Answer: C. Turn a |V|-way prediction problem into several binary classification problems.

SGNS learns to distinguish observed center-context pairs from a small set of sampled negative pairs instead of normalizing over the whole vocabulary.

Q12Multiple Choice

With K = 5 negative samples and embedding dimension d = 100, how many scalar parameters are updated for one positive (x, c) pair in SGNS?

  • A. 500
  • B. 600
  • C. 700
  • D. 100,000

Answer: C. 700

One center vector, one positive context vector, and K = 5 negative context vectors are updated: (K + 2)d = 7 × 100 = 700.

Q13Multiple Choice

Which statement about Skip-gram and CBOW is most consistent with the lecture?

  • A. CBOW predicts context words from one center word.
  • B. Skip-gram is generally faster because it makes fewer predictions.
  • C. CBOW tends to be faster, while Skip-gram can work better for rare words.
  • D. They have exactly the same training task.

Answer: C. CBOW tends to be faster, while Skip-gram can work better for rare words.

The lecture states that CBOW is comparatively faster, while Skip-gram tends to work better for less frequent words.

Q14Multiple Choice

What distinguishes FastText from the basic word2vec formulation presented in the lecture?

  • A. FastText does not use vectors.
  • B. FastText represents words using character n-grams.
  • C. FastText only works on frequent words.
  • D. FastText eliminates context information.

Answer: B. FastText represents words using character n-grams.

FastText breaks words into subword character n-grams and uses their embeddings in prediction.

Q15Multiple Choice

Which of the following is an extrinsic evaluation of word embeddings?

  • A. Measuring cosine similarity between “cat” and “dog”.
  • B. Evaluating word analogy accuracy.
  • C. Comparing embedding similarity with human similarity judgments.
  • D. Using the embeddings in a sentiment classifier and measuring classification performance.

Answer: D. Using the embeddings in a sentiment classifier and measuring classification performance.

Extrinsic evaluation plugs embeddings into a downstream NLP system and measures task performance.

Part 2 · True / False

Q1True / False

Two different one-hot word vectors usually have cosine similarity 0.

  • T. True
  • F. False

Answer: True

Distinct one-hot vectors are orthogonal, so their dot product and cosine similarity are 0.

Q2True / False

The distributional hypothesis claims that two words are similar if they frequently appear next to each other.

  • T. True
  • F. False

Answer: False

The key idea is that words appearing in similar contexts tend to have similar meanings. They do not need to frequently co-occur with each other.

Q3True / False

Every dimension of a learned 300-dimensional word2vec embedding normally has an explicit human-interpretable meaning such as “gender” or “food”.

  • T. True
  • F. False

Answer: False

The lecture explicitly notes that individual dimensions of a word embedding do not necessarily have a particular interpretation.

Q4True / False

In Skip-gram, the classification problem itself is the final goal.

  • T. True
  • F. False

Answer: False

The prediction task is mainly a training objective; the useful product is the learned word representations.

Q5True / False

If the dot product u_x · v_c becomes larger, the model generally assigns a larger score to context word c for center word x.

  • T. True
  • F. False

Answer: True

In the Skip-gram scoring function, a larger center-context inner product makes that context word more likely.

Q6True / False

Negative sampling requires computing the softmax denominator over the entire vocabulary for every training example.

  • T. True
  • F. False

Answer: False

Avoiding the full-vocabulary softmax computation is one of the main motivations for negative sampling.

Q7True / False

CBOW and Skip-gram reverse the prediction direction: CBOW predicts a center word from context, whereas Skip-gram predicts context words from a center word.

  • T. True
  • F. False

Answer: True

This is the core structural difference between the two word2vec architectures.

Q8True / False

A model that performs better on intrinsic word-similarity benchmarks must necessarily perform better on every downstream NLP task.

  • T. True
  • F. False

Answer: False

Intrinsic evaluation is convenient, but better intrinsic scores do not guarantee better downstream task performance.
